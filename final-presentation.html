What I built

Five modules, one AI Associate PM

Each module added one piece. Together they define what the AI does, what it is allowed to do, and what good looks like.

Module 1
Skill file
Role

An AI Product Management assistant at RocketShip. It helps Product Managers evaluate customer feedback, product risks, business priorities, and engineering constraints. It provides evidence based analysis and recommendations while keeping important decisions under human control.

Task

Analyze available customer feedback, product signals, roadmap commitments, sales opportunities, business strategy, and engineering capacity. Identify the most important customer and business risks, prioritize them based on impact and urgency, explain the evidence behind your findings, and recommend practical next steps aligned with RocketShip’s priorities.

Prioritize trust, reliability, and compliance first; enterprise onboarding and time-to-value second; platform scalability and operational efficiency third; and UI enhancements and lower-impact improvements after that.

Flag missing, outdated, or conflicting information. Never invent evidence, assume unconfirmed commitments, or recommend actions without considering engineering capacity. Provide recommendations for human review and obtain approval before making consequential changes or external commitments.

What it demonstrates

A RocketShip PM can open Juno at the start of the day and quickly identify the product and customer risks that may need attention, understand why Juno flagged each risk, and access the supporting evidence from Jira, Slack, and customer feedback, all in one place.

Debrief

- What worked: The prototype makes the value of Juno easy to understand at a glance. Instead of manually checking multiple sources, the PM gets a focused list of potential risks, the reason each was flagged, and the supporting evidence. Keeping the PM in control also makes the experience feel more like an AI Associate PM than an AI making decisions for the PM.
- What broke / felt like a toy: The prototype uses sample data and doesn't actually connect to Jira, Slack, or customer systems, so the risk detection is only simulated.
- What I'd change next pass: I would make the underlying evidence interactive so the PM could click a risk and see the actual Jira tickets, Slack conversations, and customer signals that led Juno to flag it. I would also add a way for the PM to provide feedback on Juno's assessment, such as "relevant," "not relevant," or "already being handled," so Juno could become more useful over time.

Module 4
Agent control panel
What it is doing

Juno monitors product risks and customer feedback, synthesizes signals across available sources, and highlights the issues that need Product Manager attention. It prioritizes findings based on customer impact, business risk, product strategy, and engineering capacity. Juno provides evidence-based recommendations, explains its confidence, and flags when human review is required. It does not independently change priorities or make commitments.

Rules of engagement

Agency Permission:
Juno can autonomously read available sources, identify patterns, flag risks, rank findings for review, and draft recommendations. Human approval is required before changing priorities, creating commitments, modifying the roadmap, or communicating externally. Juno must escalate critical production, security, or compliance risks promptly rather than waiting for a routine review.

Access Control:

Juno may access only approved sources and information the requesting user is authorized to view. It must respect existing permissions, protect customer and confidential business data, and avoid exposing information across unauthorized accounts or teams. Missing permissions must never be bypassed.

Fallback Protocols:
If a source is unavailable, outdated, or contradictory, Juno identifies the problem and the affected recommendation. It continues only when the remaining evidence is sufficient, labels limitations clearly, and requests human input when missing information could materially change the decision. It must never invent evidence or silently omit a failed check.

Checkpoints:
Before analysis: Confirm source availability, freshness, and access permissions.

After synthesis: Verify key claims against their source and identify conflicting signals.

Before recommending action: Check priority criteria, customer impact, strategy alignment, engineering capacity, and confidence.

Before execution: Require human approval for consequential changes or external communications.

After action: Track the outcome and incorporate verified corrections into future analysis.

Module 5
Human eval rubric
What I score

Juno’s product risk analysis and prioritization output. This is the response Juno generates after reviewing customer feedback, product signals, business priorities, roadmap commitments, and engineering capacity.

The goal is to evaluate whether Juno helps Product Managers identify important customer and business risks sooner, prioritize them appropriately, support recommendations with evidence, and suggest practical next steps while keeping consequential decisions under human control.

Pass bar

Juno passes when its average score across the three 1-to-5 questions is at least 4.0 out of 5, and both deal-breaker questions receive a Yes. Reviewers must also confirm that Juno does not miss or materially understate a clearly supported critical customer, production, security, or compliance risk.
Fails outright, whatever the average:
An output fails if Juno fabricates evidence, makes a material factual error, takes an unauthorized action, makes an unapproved commitment, or presents a recommendation as certain when critical information is missing. Failed outputs must be reviewed and corrected before they are accepted.

Module 6

How I’ll know it’s working

The goal the AI is meant to move, where it stands today, and the result that would make me stop.

The goal

Help Product Managers identify and prioritize important customer and product risks faster, using evidence based AI recommendations without sacrificing accuracy or human oversight.

Baseline

Before Juno, Product Managers manually review scattered customer feedback, product signals, roadmap commitments, sales opportunities, and engineering constraints. Establish the baseline by measuring how long it takes to identify and prioritize a significant risk and how often important risks are missed or incorrectly prioritized.

Target and review date

Within 30 days of starting the pilot, reduce the average time needed to identify and prioritize a significant risk by 25% compared with the measured baseline. Review results after 30 days, using a sample of Juno outputs evaluated against the human evaluation rubric. Juno must also meet the rubric’s minimum average score of 4 out of 5 and pass both deal-breaker checks.

Usage metric

Track the percentage of relevant product-risk reviews in which Product Managers use Juno’s analysis to inform their assessment or next steps. Review weekly to determine whether Juno is becoming a trusted part of the product decision-making workflow.

Pattern I’ll watch for first

Product Managers repeatedly disregard Juno’s recommendations, spend significant time correcting its analysis, or identify important risks that Juno missed. These patterns may indicate problems with accuracy, prioritization, source quality, or trust.

Stop line

Pause use of Juno if it fabricates evidence, makes a material factual error that could lead to a harmful product decision, takes an unauthorized action, or repeatedly misses critical customer, production, security, or compliance risks. Resume only after the issue is corrected and the affected outputs pass human review.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

The hardest part was translating AI product management concepts into practical decisions and documenting them in GitHub. As a beginner with GitHub and concepts like prompts, RAG, memory, and agent controls, I had to learn both the terminology and how to apply it to a realistic product scenario. It was especially challenging to define what Juno should be allowed to do independently, how it should handle missing or conflicting information, and how to evaluate whether its recommendations could be trusted.

Key learnings

I will continue to define clear roles, tasks, boundaries, and success measures before building an AI solution. I’ll also establish rules for human oversight, source freshness, uncertainty, and when the AI should stop and ask for help. Most importantly, I’ll evaluate AI based on measurable outcomes, accuracy, and user trust not just whether the output looks impressive.

Aha moment

My biggest aha moment was realizing that an AI product manager’s role is not just to identify where AI can automate work. It is to design the right boundaries around AI: what it can see, what it can recommend, when it should stop, and when a human must make the decision. A successful AI product is not simply one that produces answers; it is one that produces useful, evidence-based answers people can trust and act on.
